3D rendering

Camera code on a small C++ and OpenGL engine: a look-at transform built by hand from three vectors, and a camera that keeps its target in view while it moves.

C++ · OpenGL · SFML · 2016 · solo

Look-at transform

Instead of calling glm::lookAt, the camera builds its matrix itself: forward is the normalized vector from the target to the camera, right is the cross product of world up and forward, and up is forward × right. These three vectors and the camera's position are the columns of its transform.

The camera behaviour does this every update and then moves the camera in its own space: W/S toward and away from the target, A/D sideways, Q/E up and down. Moving sideways while turning back toward the target each frame makes it circle the target. In the clip it circles the brick teapot; the monkey's rotation is a separate behaviour.

void CameraBehaviour::update(float pStep)
{
    getInput();
    glm::vec3 forward = glm::normalize(_owner->getLocalPosition() - target->getLocalPosition());
    glm::vec3 right = glm::cross(glm::vec3(0, 1, 0), forward);
    glm::vec3 up = glm::cross(forward, right);

    _owner->setTransform(
        glm::mat4(glm::vec4(right, 0), glm::vec4(up, 0), glm::vec4(forward, 0),
                  glm::vec4(_owner->getLocalPosition(), 1))
    );

    _owner->getWorldPosition();
    if (sf::Keyboard::isKeyPressed(sf::Keyboard::Key::Q)) _owner->translate(glm::vec3(0, speed, 0));
    if (sf::Keyboard::isKeyPressed(sf::Keyboard::Key::E)) _owner->translate(glm::vec3(0, -speed, 0));
    _owner->translate(glm::vec3(-inputH * speed, 0, -inputV * speed));
}
