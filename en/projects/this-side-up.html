This Side Up

A pixel-art platformer about delivering a package, made in 72 hours for Mini Jam 188 under the limitation "All in a box". There is no jump; the player moves by punching with a boxing glove and riding the recoil. I did all of the programming.

Unity · C# · FMOD · 72 hours · team of 5

Play in browser Full playthrough

Boxing-glove movement

The player aims with the mouse or the right stick and punches: the glove extends, and after a short delay the recoil launches the player the other way. A second punch in mid-air retracts the glove with a stronger kick, which works as a double jump. The glove recharges on ground contact, with a short coyote time so a punch just after walking off a ledge still registers.

The level also has sticky walls that stop the player mid-flight and let them slide down slowly, moving platforms and conveyor belts, tuned with the designers during the jam. Rotate triggers turn gravity by 90 degrees and the camera turns with it, so parts of the level are played sideways or upside down; an FMOD parameter changes the music for each orientation.

if (protracted && activateGloves.WasPressedThisFrame())
{
    // Second punch in the air: retract and kick off harder.
    retractCoroutine?.StopCoroutine();
    Retract();
    rb.AddForce(-direction * doubleJumpForce, ForceMode2D.Impulse);
}

if (canActivate && activateGloves.WasPressedThisFrame())
{
    protracted = true;
    canActivate = false;  // recharged on (coyote) ground
    retractCoroutine = Cooldown(extendedDuration, Retract);
    Cooldown(knockbackDelay, () =>
        rb.AddForce(-direction * knockbackForce, ForceMode2D.Impulse));
}

Other systems

Besides the movement I built the dialogue view, the pause menu and the FMOD audio hookup, with state and events running on Ripple so designers could wire up triggers without code.

Made with Canozalez, Chaos-1001 (art), Pierre-Henri Barralis (music & audio) and the rest of the jam team.
