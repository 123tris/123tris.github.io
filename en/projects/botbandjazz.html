BotBandJazz

A VR game made by six students in ten weeks, set in an old theatre where the player completes mini-games to bring a robot jazz band back to the stage. It was exhibited at the Dutch Game Garden and entered in the Dutch Game Awards 2017. I was the team's generalist programmer: gameplay, VR physics and tools.

Unity · C# · HTC Vive · 10 weeks · team of 6 · 2017

Teleport locomotion

The theatre is much larger than the play area, so players move by teleporting. Pointing a Vive controller draws an arc: a projectile curve traced segment by segment until it hits geometry. Only flat, walkable surfaces are valid landing spots, which keeps players out of props and off walls, and a target marker shows where the player will stand.

An instant teleport avoids the motion sickness that smooth locomotion can cause, and the arc reaches the balconies and the stage.

Trigger system for designers

So that doors, switches and puzzles did not each need a programmer, I built a trigger system the designers used directly: add a trigger to any object, choose a function from a dropdown and enter its parameters. It runs on trigger enter or on collision.

The designers set up the interactions themselves while I worked on the systems underneath.

Rhythm mini-game editor

One of the mini-games is a rhythm game played to the band's music. Notes are placed in beats rather than seconds: in the editor I built inside Unity, the designer sets the song's BPM and notes snap to a beat grid aligned with the track.

A year later I built a standalone rhythm-game editor, the osu!catch level editor.

Made by a team of six students at Saxion University of Applied Sciences.
