Game physics

2D physics on a vector class I wrote from scratch: a ball launched like a slingshot that falls under gravity and bounces off walls, and a tank that steers and aims with vector angles. In Unity I also wrote a separate test of axis-aligned bounding box collision with custom rigidbodies, outside Unity's own physics.

C# · GXPEngine · Unity · 2016 · solo

Wall collisions

Each step adds gravity to the velocity and the velocity to the position. When the ball has passed a wall, it is moved back along its velocity by the fraction of the step that went past the wall, so it bounces from the point of impact instead of from inside the wall. The velocity component into the wall is then inverted. The excerpt shows the left wall; the other three work the same way.

Dragging pulls the ball back with a square-root falloff, and releasing it launches the ball the opposite way and switches gravity on. The red trail runs from the previous position to the current one each frame; the green arrow is the velocity.

Steering with vector angles

The tank's velocity vector is rotated together with the tank, so it always drives along its body. The barrel is a child sprite whose angle is the angle of the vector from the tank to the mouse, minus the body's rotation. Bullets take the barrel's angle and spawn in front of the barrel.

AABB collision in Unity

I also wrote a separate test in Unity that doesn't use Unity's physics. Each box takes its extents from its position and its BoxCollider's size, and two boxes overlap only if their extents overlap on x, y and z. Custom rigidbodies integrate gravity and velocity in FixedUpdate, and a physics manager tests every rigidbody against the static objects and reverses its velocity when they overlap.
